Does increased spending for health services mean improved life expectancy?

Exploring 10 selected OECD countries from 2015 to 2024, to analyse how healthcare spending correlates with life expectancy. National expenditures, life expectancy data and shifts over time can be interactively explored.

Questions We Explore

These five questions about healthcare costs and life expectancy in the selected OECD countries are explored. Select the card for a summary.

01

Do countries that spend more on healthcare tend to have longer life expectancy?

Investigate the relationship →

Countries that spend more on healthcare generally achieve longer life expectancies, though the relationship is not proportional, with the difference approximately around three years. However, the United States spends substantially more than any other country while recording a lower life expectancy.

02

How does healthcare spending per person differ across the selected OECD countries?

Compare spending →

Healthcare spending varies across the selected OECD countries. The United States records the highest expenditure per person, while countries such as Korea, New Zealand and Italy generally spend less.

03

What is the life expectancy in each of the selected OECD countries?

Explore life expectancy →

Life expectancy ranges from approximately the high-70s to mid-80s years. Japan and Korea generally achieve the highest values.

04

How have healthcare expenditure and life expectancy changed between 2015 and 2024?

Explore trends →

Healthcare expenditure increased across all countries between 2015 and 2024. Life expectancy generally increased over the decade, despite temporary declines around the COVID pandemic in 2020–2021.

05

Which countries have a relatively long life expectancy compared to their healthcare spending?

Compare outcomes →

Japan, Korea and some European countries achieved comparatively high life expectancy despite lower healthcare spending than the United States, indicating stronger outcomes relative to expenditure.
